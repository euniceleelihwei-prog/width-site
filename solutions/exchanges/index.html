Sound Familiar?

01

Too Many Deposit Alerts for the Review Team

If a tool only asks "are these addresses connected?", ordinary deposits get traced a few hops out to something risky, and most alerts end up as false positives.

How DART solves it

  • A hop only counts if the funds arrived before they left. Paths that run backwards in time are dropped
  • Share of funds matters: the further the hop, the higher the threshold, so a trace amount of bad money doesn't condemn an address
  • Four dispositions (pass, review, EDD, block) mean reviewers only see the middle two

ProofIn one payment company's test, another tool rated an address "medium-low risk, needs review". DART scored it 0. Broken down transfer by transfer, the risky funds could not have reached it in time.

02

Getting Dragged In by Other Exchanges' Users

A hot wallet touches thousands of addresses a day. Trace through another exchange and you will almost always hit something risky.

How DART solves it

  • Exchange counterparties are exempt by default: tracing stops at another exchange, so other users of that exchange don't count against you
  • Switch the exemption off for strict scenarios such as sanctions screening

ProofA Middle East exchange runs deposit and withdrawal screening with the exemption on by default.

03

New Regulatory Actions Outpace Your Rules

Official lists and enforcement orders change faster every year. Maintaining rules in-house is slow and easy to get wrong.

How DART solves it

  • We turn new official actions into built-in rulesets you can select in the console or API
  • Every rule is readable. Copy a ruleset and adjust it to your own policy

ProofFinCEN's 9714 order was published on 6 November. The DART built-in ruleset went live the same day.

FAQ

Questions Exchanges Ask

Does the exchange exemption let risk slip through?

The exemption only stops tracing once a path reaches another exchange. If the screened address carries a risk label itself, or a path hits a rule before it reaches an exchange, it still counts. Turn the exemption off for strict scenarios such as sanctions screening.

Should deposits and withdrawals use the same rules?

We don't recommend it. Deposits look at where funds came from (inbound) and withdrawals at where they go (outbound). DART ships separate KYT-IN and KYT-OUT rulesets. Hops default to 3 inbound and 1 outbound, and both are adjustable.

Will screening slow down crediting?

Sanctions checks return in under a second, and sync calls return the result directly. Deep traces on very active addresses take longer, so use async mode and either credit then act on the result, or hold then release.

Do we have to add rules when a new list is published?

No. We turn new official actions into built-in rulesets. The FinCEN 9714 order, for example, was live the day it was published. You can still copy and edit any ruleset.

What can we show an examiner?

Every screening has an immutable snapshot recording the request and the full result. Pull them in bulk over the API to report on any period. The product manual documents rule definitions and the scoring formula, so it can sit alongside your internal policy.

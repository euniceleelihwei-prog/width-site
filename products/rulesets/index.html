Rulesets & scoring

Your Risk Appetite, Written as Rules

Which money trails count as a violation, how many points each one costs, and where you draw the line differs for every institution. DART splits this into two rulesets. The path-matching ruleset decides whether a trail is a violation. The scoring ruleset decides how serious it is and what to do. Both are open, and both are yours to edit.

The Problems with Someone Else's Rules

01

How DART solves it

How DART solves it

Rules come in three shapes: self-hit (the address carries the label), direct exposure (one hop), and layered exposure (risky funds still above the threshold after several hops).

Scoring ruleset

Where the Score Comes from, in One Formula

The scoring ruleset is a matrix of direction × hop band × severity. Each hit contributes:

base points × severity weight × share of funds

The share is measured against the address's total onchain flow, not just the part of the graph that was expanded. Hits add up, cap at 100, and map to one of four verdicts.

  • Default severity weights: critical 1, high 0.8, medium 0.6, low 0.3
  • A self-hit overrides the sum with the self-hit score (100 by default): block
  • Run the same hits through a different scoring ruleset and the score changes; the hits don't

FAQ

Rulesets and Scoring Questions

What is the difference between the path-matching and scoring rulesets?

The path-matching ruleset only answers whether a money trail is a violation and which rule it breaks. It never produces a score. The score comes entirely from the scoring ruleset, so the same hits can score differently under a different scoring ruleset while the hits themselves stay the same.

Can I edit the built-in rulesets?

Built-ins are read-only so every customer has a stable baseline. Clone one and you can change anything in your copy; each ruleset is stored separately.

What are the verdict bands?

By default 0–19 pass, 20–49 review, 50–79 enhanced due diligence and 80–100 block. You can move the bands to match your risk appetite.

Why do further hops need a bigger share?

The further a hop is from the screened address, the more hands the money has passed through and the weaker the link. By default risky funds must reach 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% of the flow at hops 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 before they count, so an address that brushed against a little tainted money isn't treated like the source.

Can an AI assistant edit rules for me?

Yes. DART's MCP tools cover reading and writing rulesets and rules, so you can describe a policy in plain language and have your assistant draft or adjust the rules.

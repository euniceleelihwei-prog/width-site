What address screening has to get right

Why Screening One Address Is Harder Than It Looks

01

Everything Comes Back "Linked to Risk"

Follow any busy address far enough and you will touch a risky one. A yes/no link tells you nothing.

How DART solves it

  • A hop only counts if the funds arrived before they left. Paths that run backwards in time are dropped
  • The further away, the higher the bar: by default risky funds must make up 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% of the flow at hops 2 / 3 / 4 / 5
  • Exchange counterparties are exempt by default

ProofA payment company ran the same address through another tool and DART. The other tool said "medium-low risk, review". DART scored it 0. Broken down transfer by transfer, the risky funds could not have reached it in time.

02

You Checked One Chain, the Trouble Was on Another

An EVM address is the same wallet on every EVM chain. Clean on Ethereum says nothing about the rest.

How DART solves it

  • All chains in one call: DART finds every chain and token where the address has stablecoin activity and screens each one
  • The worst result comes back on top, so you see at once where the risk sits
  • Or narrow it to specific chains, or screen USDT and USDC on one chain together

Proof11 chains covered. Chains with no activity are skipped and not charged.

03

You Have a Score but Can't Say Why

How DART solves it

  • Every hit is listed: rule, direction, hop, amount, share of funds, points added
  • Excluded paths show their reason too: backwards in time, share too small, exchange exemption
  • Every screening is saved as an immutable snapshot

ProofPull the snapshot when a reviewer or regulator asks how a decision was made.

How it works

Three Ways an Address Can Be Hit, Checked in One Pass

Risk can sit on the address itself or on the money that passed through it. DART looks at both inflow (where funds came from) and outflow (where they went), with separate weights for each direction.

  1. Self-hit

    The Address Carries a Risk Label

    It is on a sanctions list, frozen by a stablecoin issuer, or belongs to a hacker or mixer. A self-hit scores 100 by default and is a block.

  2. Direct exposure

    It Dealt with a Risky Address in One Hop

    It received from, or sent to, a risky address directly. Scored by amount and share of funds.

  3. Layered exposure

    Enough Risky Funds Survive Several Hops

    Funds passed through intermediaries, in valid time order, and still clear that hop's threshold. Hops are configurable; we suggest 3 inbound and 1 outbound for daily screening.

API

FAQ

Address Screening Questions

What is the difference between KYA and KYT?

KYA looks at an address as a whole, which suits onboarding, whitelisting and checks before you accept funds. KYT looks at one specific deposit or withdrawal and asks where that money came from or where it is going. Both run on the same data and rules engine and are often used together.

Which chains and tokens are supported?

DART focuses on USDT and USDC and currently covers 11 chains. The live list is available from the API, and new chains become available automatically.

How long does a screening take?

Sanctions checks return in under a second. Most full address screenings come back within a minute; very active addresses traced deep take longer, and async mode keeps your flow moving while they run.

Will an address be flagged just because it used an exchange?

No. Exchange counterparties are exempt by default, so a path stops at the exchange instead of blaming your user for what other customers of that exchange did. You can switch this off for strict sanctions screening.

Can I change the thresholds?

Yes. Verdict bands, rule points and per-hop thresholds can all be tuned to your risk appetite. Built-in rulesets are read-only; clone one and edit the copy.
